Single Patient Prediction

Step-by-Step Guide

  • Age: Patient's age in years (required)
  • Gender: Male or Female

  • AFP (ng/mL): Alpha-fetoprotein level (required) - main tumor marker
  • Albumin (g/dL): Serum albumin - liver synthetic function
  • Total Bilirubin: Liver excretory function
  • ALT/AST (U/L): Liver inflammation markers
Tip: AFP > 400 ng/mL is a significant risk factor for recurrence.

  • Major Tumor Dimension (cm): Largest tumor diameter (required)
  • Number of Nodules: Total tumor count
Note: Tumors > 5 cm are associated with higher recurrence risk.

  • Cirrhosis: Presence of liver cirrhosis
  • Hepatitis B (HBsAg): Active hepatitis B infection
  • Hepatitis C (HCVAb): Hepatitis C antibody status
  • Diabetes: Diabetes mellitus
  • Alcohol/Smoking: Lifestyle risk factors

After submitting, you'll receive:

  • Risk Probability: Percentage likelihood of recurrence
  • Risk Level: Low, Medium, or High classification
  • SHAP Analysis: Visual explanation of contributing factors
  • Recommendations: Clinical follow-up guidelines

Understanding SHAP Explanations

What is SHAP?

SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) is a method to explain machine learning predictions. It shows how each patient characteristic (feature) contributes to the final risk assessment.

How to Read the SHAP Chart
Red Bars

Features that increase recurrence risk. Longer red bars mean stronger positive contribution to risk.

Green Bars

Features that decrease recurrence risk. Longer green bars mean stronger protective effect.

Example Interpretation
Feature SHAP Value Direction Interpretation
AFP = 850 ng/mL +0.24 ↑ Risk Very high AFP strongly increases predicted risk
Cirrhosis = Present +0.18 ↑ Risk Underlying cirrhosis adds significant risk
Albumin = 4.2 g/dL -0.10 ↓ Risk Good liver function provides some protection
Base Value Explained

The base value shown on the chart represents the average prediction across all patients in the training data. Think of it as the "starting point" before considering this specific patient's characteristics.

Calculation: Final Probability = Base Value + Sum of all SHAP values

Risk Level Stratification

Low Risk (<40%)
Recommended Actions:
  • Annual surveillance imaging
  • AFP testing every 6 months
  • Routine follow-up visits
  • Continue current liver disease management
Medium Risk (40-70%)
Recommended Actions:
  • Imaging every 3-6 months
  • Quarterly AFP monitoring
  • Regular hepatologist follow-up
  • Address modifiable risk factors
High Risk (>70%)
Recommended Actions:
  • Urgent imaging within 4-6 weeks
  • Monthly AFP monitoring
  • Multidisciplinary team review
  • Consider adjuvant therapy
  • Evaluate transplant eligibility

Frequently Asked Questions

The system achieves approximately 85-90% accuracy with an ROC-AUC of 0.86-0.94 depending on the data. However, this is a decision support tool and should not replace clinical judgment. Predictions should be considered alongside all available clinical information.

The system can handle missing values by using default values (typically 0 or population averages). However, for the most accurate predictions, provide as much data as possible. Required fields (Age, AFP, Major_Dim) must be filled.

SHAP values are calculated for each individual patient. The same feature may have different impacts depending on its value and the combination of other features. For example, AFP of 500 ng/mL will have higher impact than AFP of 50 ng/mL.

This tool is designed for clinical decision support, not replacement of clinical judgment. Always consider predictions in the context of:
  • Complete patient history
  • Physical examination findings
  • Additional imaging or tests
  • Patient preferences and goals of care
  • Institutional protocols

The model can be retrained with new data by re-running the training pipeline. It's recommended to retrain periodically as new patient outcomes become available to maintain accuracy.
Important Disclaimer

This prediction system is intended for educational and research purposes and to support clinical decision-making. It should not be used as the sole basis for patient care decisions. Always consult with qualified healthcare professionals for medical advice and treatment decisions.
